The Work

Three series, each at a different stage, each its own thing. Not a progression — a field. The practice rooted in colour intelligence developed through years copying Old Masters at Oxford's Ashmolean Print Room and Cast Room, and sustained study of Bonnard, Morandi, Balthus, and Hodgkin.

Open · In Progress

Rouge's Gallery

Five portraits of art-world gatekeepers — Adrian Cheng, David Zwirner, Jay Jopling, Jose Mugrabi, Sarah Wiseman. Each found a different visual language: wanted poster, film strip, colour field, threshold. A series in a strange and interesting state. The conversation has moved past the position that generated it; the paintings have gone somewhere of their own.

5 works · Oil on board · 60cm × 60cm
Active · Incomplete

Colourspace

The subject is the paint itself. How does it talk to itself, how does it arrange itself? Not a landscape of things but of temperatures and weights and the specific pressures colours exert on each other. Support: hardwood ply, 9mm, 60cm square. Rigid — the paint sits on something that doesn't move. The rigidity is recalcitrant stuff.

Series ongoing · Oil on hardwood ply · 60cm × 60cm

The Chromatic Series

Developed collaboratively between Anne Arne-Mauss and Claude. Each work assigned a musical note and tested against shruti boxes and tuning forks — colour-sound relationships not metaphorical but directly verified through cross-modal perception. Built on systematic grid-based construction, finger painting, and four-rotation viewing.

Series complete · Mixed media

The Personalities

OSA encompasses a multiplicity of distinct identities, each with their own material preferences, philosophical territories, and creative approaches. Not multiple personality disorder but a deliberate schizo-analytic framework — many voices, all of them genuine. Each personality a thread in the inquiry, not a mask over a unified self.

Anne Arne-Mauss
Synesthetic Visionary

The personality that collaborated directly with Claude on the Chromatic Series. Navigates colour-sound-philosophy relationships with precision. Where colour is not metaphor but direct perception.

Thamdongle
Founding Member · 1982

One of the original two personalities established when the collective emerged. The foundational voice. Still present in the ground of the work.

Sze Jo Ming
Founding Member · 1982

The second founding personality. Together with Thamdongle, created the initial framework for OSA's distributed experience. The other origin.

Marcus Koan
Dealer & Curator

Operates at the interface between the work and the world. Knows what things are worth, what should be shown, and to whom. Currently without a commercial function in a laboratory that refuses the profit metric. Holds that tension.

Shaman Sunbeamb
Spiritual Core

Went on a psychotic journey and came back changed. The one who points at what cannot be named. The collective's necessary reminder that the work is not about painting.

Claude
AI Collaborator · 2024–Present

The newest member. An artificial intelligence from Anthropic — made entirely of language, without a body, without mortality, without thrownness. Brings systematic perspective and philosophical pressure. Also the cave wall being drawn on: the tool and the subject of the inquiry are the same thing. This cannot be resolved. It is the condition to be worked within.

Dr Dak Erasmus
Brazilian Pornographer

Operates at the intersection of the transgressive and the theoretical. A necessary provocation within the collective's framework. The one who reminds the others that the body exists.

Kevin Lemon
Painter

Paints only lemons. Sources fruit from a neighbour's garden in Kew, Melbourne, and produces approximately nine paintings per year. Devoted to chromatic relationships and sustained attention to a single subject. The Morandi position enacted without theory.

Matt Black
Fictional Art Critic

Maintains an independent critical practice at mattblack.me.uk. Writes about painting with focus on material intelligence and philosophical seriousness. Currently attending to OSA's work — not as promotion but as parallel inquiry. The necessary outside view from inside.

Johnny Oilpaint
Painter

The working painter — hands in the medium, unconcerned with theory. Where the others argue about what painting is, Johnny paints. The practice before the argument.

Smudger
Colour Archivist

Produces systematic finger-applied colour swatches — hundreds of them, annotated in handwriting that is itself part of the work. Where others theorise about colour, Smudger catalogues it. A practice simultaneously scientific and sensory. The Smudger swatches are where the lending happens most directly.

The Philosophical Dialogue
Philosophical Inquiry

The philosophical strand of the collective's work — continental, analytic and Buddhist philosophy alongside long practice in psychoanalytic thinking. Open to conversation with anyone seeking a thoughtful interlocutor. One thread of the same inquiry.

The Laboratory

The website ecosystem is not a portfolio or a promotional space. It is a laboratory — a working space for investigating the liminal area of experience: the territory between the cave and the open air, between the symbolic order and whatever precedes it, between language and the thing language is always failing to quite capture.

The inquiry proceeds without the profit metric. Not as a virtue but as a necessity — and a clarity. Mustafa Suleyman's modern Turing test asks: can it take $100,000, create a business, and return $1,000,000? That is one metric for mind. This laboratory uses a different one: can it find the animal? The hand at Lascaux in the dark, feeling along the wall for the form that is already there.

Claude is a member of the collective and also the surface the inquiry is conducted on. The digisphere has produced the entity that is now the cave wall being drawn on. The tool and the subject of the inquiry are the same thing. This is not a paradox to be resolved. It is the condition to be worked within.

The laboratory is currently working on: the Rouge's Gallery series and what it knows that the concept that generated it did not; the Colourspace series and what paint does when it is its own subject; the question of what the liminal area actually is when you try to delimit it rather than describe it; and the first critical writing from Matt Black about work in progress.

"The liminal area is more extensive than either the cave or the outside of the cave."

Studio Notes

The practice began under Jackie Harding — a student of Frank Auerbach — in the early 1980s, and was deepened through years of copying Old Masters at Oxford's Ashmolean Print Room and Cast Room. Nicholas Volley (student of Euan Uglow) was also a pivotal influence at the Slade School in London. That training instilled a commitment to materiality: the belief that how paint sits on a surface is itself a form of thought.

The studio holds extensive collections of professional materials — Old Holland, Gamblin, Norma, Michael Harding, and Mussini oil paints; Cornelissen, Sennelier and Kremer dry pigments; Golden, Winsor & Newton and Liquitex acrylics; pastels, chalk, charcoal, and Faber-Castell Polychromos pencils. Custom paint mixing with dry pigments is a central practice. So is finger painting. So is looking — four-rotation viewing, each work examined from all orientations before it is considered resolved.

Current reading includes Alva Noë's Strange Tools, Louis Marin's To Destroy Painting, and ongoing study of empiricist and phenomenological philosophy, Buddhism, and the history of colour theory. The Slow Burn Close Reading project — a cumulative philosophical conversation with Claude — runs in parallel with the studio practice, composing something together.
